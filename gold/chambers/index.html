01 / THE LOCAL ADVANTAGE

A room full of businesses.
A community of possibilities.

Membership is a starting point. The value grows when you participate, contribute, and become a familiar face.

Connections

Meet owners, professionals, and community leaders in a setting built for conversation. Ask who they serve and what they are working on.

Visibility

Explore member directories, event introductions, newsletters, and sponsorship opportunities that fit your audience.

Credibility

Build a reputation through consistency: show up, be helpful, and keep your promises. Let people get to know your work.

Learning

Use workshops and peer conversations to understand local business priorities and improve the questions you ask.

Community

Support a committee, volunteer project, or local initiative. Shared work gives relationships time to develop.

Introductions

Learn which professionals complement your work. Ask permission before making a thoughtful introduction.

MIKE’S FIELD LESSON

Make the chamber calendar part of your monthly routine. Use the public member directory to understand the local business community, then show up ready to listen.

03 / FIND YOUR ROOM

Start nearby.
Choose the right conversation.

Look for the audience and format that suit your work. You can explore guest attendance before making a membership decision.

Chamber mixers & breakfasts

Check the event calendar and guest policy. Ask the host which events bring together owners or professionals in your field.

Focused networking groups

Explore BNI chapters, Rotary clubs, and industry associations. Ask about category availability, membership expectations, and attendance rules.

Workshops & business expos

Look at Small Business Development Center workshops, trade shows, and local lunch-and-learns. A shared topic makes introductions easier.

Find your next local gathering.

Search a city or area, then confirm the organizer’s current date, venue, fees, and guest policy.

Before you join

Ask: Are my intended contacts active here? Can I attend as a guest? What does membership include? Which time commitments and fees apply?

Use the directory thoughtfully

Research a few businesses and learn what they do. A public listing helps you prepare; it does not establish permission for bulk outreach.

04 / YOUR PRE-EVENT ROUTINE

Walk in prepared.
Stay present with people.

Five practical checks, adapted from the Fast Start event routine.

ARRIVE ABOUT 10 MINUTES EARLY

Meet the host.
Find a natural opening.

Introduce yourself, thank the organizer, and ask who is new. A small conversation before the room fills can give you a comfortable place to start.

“What brought you here today, and what are you focused on in your business?”

Choose a manageable goal: a few meaningful conversations, one helpful introduction, and clear follow-through.

05 / CONVERSATIONS THAT FEEL HUMAN

The right words.
In your own voice.

Choose a situation, add the details you actually know, and copy a draft. Review all placeholders before using it.

A little context makes it yours.

The drafts update as you type. Nothing is saved or sent.

MEETING A BUSINESS OWNER

Hi, I’m [Your Name] with Dreams Business Resources. I help local business owners explore resources for business expenses, employee benefits, and growth. What are you focused on in your business right now?

Start with their priority. A relevant question is more useful than listing every service.

WHEN THEY ASK WHAT YOU DO

We start with a Dreams Score overview to organize questions about areas such as expenses, payroll, benefits, risk, and cash flow. It helps us decide what may deserve a closer look. If a topic fits, we can connect you with a specialist to review the details. Any recommendations depend on your business and the current program terms. Would you like me to send the overview?

Offer a useful review without promising findings, eligibility, or savings.

TURNING INTEREST INTO A CONVERSATION

Hi [First Name], I enjoyed hearing about [Shared Detail]. Would a short coffee conversation be useful so I can understand your priorities a little better? We could focus on the one area you mentioned and decide whether there is a resource worth exploring. Would [Day / Time] work?

Tie the invitation to something they raised. Confirm the time and purpose together.

A PERSONAL FOLLOW-UP

Subject: Good meeting you at [Event] Hi [First Name], It was good connecting through the Chamber. I enjoyed our conversation about [Shared Detail]. Here is the [resource/link] I promised. If you would like to explore the Dreams Score overview, we can use it to identify questions about your business priorities and decide whether a specialist conversation would be useful. Would you like to set aside a few minutes? Best, [Your Name]

Send the promised resource first. Confirm the current review offering before describing its cost or scope.

A LIGHT, RELEVANT FOLLOW-UP

Subject: Following up on [Shared Detail] Hi [First Name], Your comment about [Shared Detail] stayed with me. I have a resource on [Topic] that may be relevant. Would you like me to send it over? Best, [Your Name]

One reason to reach out. One easy question. Respect a no or a request to stop.

INVITE PEOPLE TO SOMETHING USEFUL

Subject: Chamber lunch & learn: [Topic] Hi [First Name], I’m hosting a practical conversation for local business owners about [Topic]. We’ll share an overview, discuss useful questions to ask, and leave time for conversation. Location: [Venue] Date / time: [Date / Time] Cost and lunch arrangements: [Confirmed details] Would you like me to save you a seat? Best, [Your Name]

Confirm the host, specialist, venue, topic, and costs before sending. Only promise lunch if it is arranged.

EXPLORE A MEMBER EDUCATION SESSION

Subject: An education idea for your members Hi [First Name], I’d like to learn which business questions your members are bringing to you most often. We may be able to support a practical session on [Topic], with a qualified specialist available for questions. Could we schedule 20 minutes to discuss the audience, useful outcomes, and your requirements for presenters? Best, [Your Name]

Begin with member needs. Discuss a specific program or partnership only when it is relevant.

KEEP THE CONVERSATION COMFORTABLE

Acknowledge.
Then give them room.

Useful responses to common situations.

“I already have an adviser.”

“That makes sense. What we discuss can be shared with your existing adviser. Is there a question you would like another resource on?”

“I’m too busy right now.”

“Understood. Would you prefer I send a short overview, or leave it for another time?”

“What will this save me?”

“I would not want to guess. A qualified specialist would need to review your situation and the current program details first.”

06 / AFTER THE HANDSHAKE

Turn a good conversation
into a kept promise.

A practical follow-up rhythm. Match the timing and channel to what you agreed.

  1. Capture the context

    After the conversation, note the name, business, event, shared detail, and permission to follow up. Record whether they are a potential client or a potential alliance partner.

  2. Deliver what you promised

    Aim to send the agreed resource the same day or next business day. Mention the event and one real detail so they recognize the conversation.

  3. Agree on a next step

    If they show interest, choose a specific conversation or introduction. If you follow up again, add useful context and respect their preference.

  4. Stay part of the community

    Attend consistently, contribute to a committee, share relevant resources, and close the loop on introductions. Keep notes so the next conversation builds on the last.

Give every connection a little context.

Add the person to your database, note where you met, and keep the promised next step visible.

07 / CHAMBER & STRATEGIC ALLIANCES

Help the chamber
help its members.

Work with leadership on a specific member need. Keep this partnership conversation separate from your conversations with individual business owners.

THE ORGANIZATION

For chamber leadership

Explore relevant education, useful member resources, and a manageable pilot. Ask how the chamber measures member value and chooses partners.

THE MEMBERS

For member businesses

Offer clear explanations, a voluntary next step, and access to a qualified specialist. Let each business decide whether the subject fits its needs.

THE PROVIDER

For the delivery team

Agree on the presenter, approved materials, responsibilities, onboarding support, and how questions will be answered after the event.

A 20-MINUTE LEADERSHIP CONVERSATION

Listen first.
Propose a small pilot.

  1. Minutes 0–5 · Understand

    Which questions come up most often? Who would benefit from a session?

  2. Minutes 5–10 · Shape the idea

    Choose one topic and describe a useful takeaway. Ask about presenter and sponsorship requirements.

  3. Minutes 10–15 · Define delivery

    Confirm a specialist, date, invitation process, and opt-in follow-up plan.

  4. Minutes 15–20 · Agree on next steps

    Name an owner for each action and a date to review the proposal.

QUANTUM 125 / BENEFITS PARTNERSHIP

Explore the opportunity.
Get the details in writing.

The Fast Start lesson introduces a possible chamber benefits partnership. Use it as a discussion topic with the current provider and chamber leadership.

  • Confirm participating-chamber eligibility and current employee minimums.
  • Request the current program documents, costs, benefits, limitations, and a business-specific illustration.
  • Ask how any proposed chamber compensation is calculated, disclosed, and documented.
  • Confirm onboarding, member support, data handling, and who answers benefits or tax questions.
  • Agree on approved marketing, voluntary participation, and how results will be reviewed.

Employee minimums, savings figures, and compensation examples in older training are not promises. Verify current terms with the provider and appropriate advisers before presenting them.

MAKE THE FIRST OFFER PRACTICAL

A lunch & learn
worth making time for.

Choose one topic members have asked about. Coordinate with the chamber and bring a specialist when the subject requires one.

5 minutes · Set the context

Explain the question the session will answer. Ask attendees what would make the time useful.

15 minutes · Teach one thing

Share a clear framework or checklist. Use realistic examples with assumptions stated, then let a qualified presenter handle technical questions.

10 minutes · Questions & next steps

Leave room for discussion. Offer an optional resource or review and obtain permission for any follow-up.

Possible topics: organizing business expenses, preparing better questions for a benefits review, or building a consistent referral process. Confirm the session length and format with the host.
