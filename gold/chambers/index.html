01 / FIND YOUR ROOM

Start local.
Show up with purpose.

Find an event where the people you want to understand already gather.

Your local Chamber

Explore its calendar, member directory, and guest events. Check membership fees and attendance requirements before joining.

A focused business group

Look for a relevant trade association, referral group, Rotary gathering, or industry breakfast. Learn the group’s expectations before participating.

A shared learning experience

Consider a business expo, small-business workshop, or a lunch-and-learn. A useful topic gives people a natural reason to talk.

Find a room near you.

Enter a city to search public event calendars. Check the organizer’s current date, location, and guest policy.

02 / BEFORE YOU WALK IN

A little preparation.
A better conversation.

Have the basics ready so you can focus on the person in front of you.

YOUR FIVE-MINUTE RESET

Be a familiar face.
Become a useful connection.

Arrive a little early. Introduce yourself to the host. Ask people what they are working on, listen for a real need, and connect them with something useful when you can.

“What brought you here today, and what are you focused on in your business?”

You don’t need to meet everyone. Leave with a few conversations you can remember and a next step you can keep.

03 / FIND YOUR WORDS

Start with curiosity.
Keep it personal.

Use these as starting points. Offer the relevant resource and let the other person decide what comes next.

A little context makes it yours.

The drafts update as you type. Nothing is saved or sent.

AT THE EVENT

Hi, I’m [Your Name] with Dreams Business Resources. I help business owners connect with resources for business costs, employee benefits, and growth. What are you focused on in your business right now?

Pause after the question. Listen for their priorities before describing a service.

THE VALUE CONVERSATION

We use the Dreams Score overview to start a conversation about areas such as expenses, cash flow, benefits, and risk. If one area is worth a closer look, we can bring in the right specialist. Would it be useful if I sent you the overview?

Describe a review as an opportunity to learn. Findings and eligibility depend on the business.

A PERSONAL FOLLOW-UP

Subject: Good meeting you at [Event] Hi [First Name], I enjoyed our conversation about [Shared Detail]. Here is the [resource/link] I promised. If it would be useful, I’d be glad to set aside a few minutes to hear more about your priorities and see whether there is a relevant next step. Best, [Your Name]

Name the actual event, replace the resource placeholder, and use the channel they agreed to.

AN INVITATION WITH A PURPOSE

Subject: A practical business conversation over lunch Hi [First Name], I’m putting together a small lunch-and-learn for local business owners about [Topic]. We’ll share a useful overview, discuss common questions, and leave time for conversation. Location: [Venue] Date and time: [Date / Time] Cost: [Confirmed cost or complimentary] Would you like the details? [Your Name]

Confirm the venue, host, topic, and cost before sending. Invite people for whom the subject is relevant.

04 / A BIGGER COMMUNITY CONVERSATION

Help the Chamber
help its members.

A strategic alliance starts with what the organization and its members actually need.

A conversation with leadership.

Ask the director which issues members bring up most often. Explore whether an educational session or a small pilot review would add useful member value.

“Would it make sense to explore a practical session for your members on the business questions they are already asking?”
Explore the Partner Program →

Bring questions, then build the plan.

  • Who is the right audience, and what would make a session useful?
  • Which qualified specialist should answer program questions?
  • What eligibility, fees, benefits, and participation terms apply today?
  • How will introductions, member permission, and follow-up work?
  • If a revenue-sharing arrangement is proposed, what do the written agreement and professional rules allow?

For Quantum or other benefits programs, confirm the current provider requirements before quoting employee minimums, savings, or Chamber compensation.
