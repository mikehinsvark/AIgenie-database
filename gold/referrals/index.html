01 / A SIMPLE REFERRAL SYSTEM

Make introductions
easy to give and receive.

Create the conditions for a good introduction. The relationship determines whether and when it happens.

01 / IDENTIFY

Know who you help.

Choose a specific kind of business, concern, and person who can make the decision.

02 / EQUIP

Give people the words.

Prepare a one-sentence description and a short introduction they can forward.

03 / ASK

Choose the right moment.

After delivering value, ask whether one relevant person comes to mind.

04 / APPRECIATE

Close the loop.

Thank the person for the introduction, whether or not it becomes business.

A clear description is easier to remember.

“I help business owners connect with specialists when they want to review business costs, employee benefits, or potential tax-credit opportunities.”

02 / STAY CONNECTED

Care for the relationship
between the opportunities.

Build habits you can sustain. A genuine check-in should feel relevant to the person receiving it.

Remember the person

Keep a detail from your conversation: a business milestone, a shared interest, or a priority they mentioned.

Bring useful context

Share a resource, make a relevant introduction, or ask how an important project is going.

Show appreciation

Write a personal thank-you. Recognize the effort behind an introduction, not just the commercial result.

BUILD YOUR RELATIONSHIP LIST

Your database is
your starting point.

Think about clients and past customers, friends, former coworkers, vendors, neighbors, business owners, and professional advisers. Give every relationship context before deciding how to reconnect.

Find the people you know →

Make the introduction comfortable.

  • Ask both people whether an introduction is welcome.
  • Explain the specific reason you think they should meet.
  • Share only the contact details and context they agreed to share.
  • Make the next action easy to understand.
  • Thank the introducer and keep them appropriately informed.
03 / THE WORDS TO MAKE IT EASY

A thoughtful ask.
A generous thank-you.

Choose the moment and customize the message. Keep the introduction voluntary.

A little context makes it yours.

The drafts update as you type. Nothing is saved or sent.

AFTER YOU HAVE DELIVERED VALUE

Hi [First Name], I’ve appreciated working with you on [Shared Detail]. Does one business owner come to mind who is dealing with [specific concern] and might welcome a conversation like the one we had? If so, would you feel comfortable asking whether they would like an introduction? [Your Name]

Be specific about whom you can help. Give the person room to say no.

MAKE THE HANDOFF EASY

Hi [Contact Name], I thought of you because [specific reason]. I know [Your Name] through [relationship], and they help business owners connect with resources around [relevant topic]. Would you like me to introduce you? I’m happy to connect you if it would be useful.

Give this to the introducer as an editable draft. Let them describe their own experience honestly.

RECOGNIZE THE TRUST

Hi [First Name], Thank you for introducing me to [Contact Name]. I appreciate the trust behind that introduction. I’ll follow through thoughtfully and keep you appropriately updated. [Your Name]

Thank them even if the timing or fit does not lead to business.

A HANDWRITTEN NOTE

Dear [First Name], Thank you for your time today. I enjoyed hearing about [Shared Detail] and learning more about what you are building. I look forward to staying in touch and following through on the next step we discussed. Warm regards, [Your Name]

Use a real detail and your own words. A short, specific note is enough.

APPRECIATE THE RELATIONSHIP

Dear [First Name], Thank you for the confidence you have placed in me. I appreciate the opportunity to work together on [Shared Detail]. I look forward to earning that trust through useful work and consistent follow-through. Warm regards, [Your Name]

Match the note to the actual relationship and the work you have agreed to do.
