01 / THE FOUNDATION

Small habits.
Steady momentum.

Choose a few repeatable activities before adding more tools or paid campaigns.

Build your warm database

Start with people you know. Add the business, where you met, and a detail that will help your next conversation.

Share something useful

Plan two or three thoughtful social posts each week. Answer a real business question or share a relevant learning.

Get out into your community

Put local visits or a networking event on your calendar. Match the time and setting to the people you want to meet.

Follow through consistently

Keep the promise you made, record the response, and agree on a sensible next step. Quality matters more than a fixed touch count.

Make introductions easy

After delivering value, ask for a specific introduction. Give people clear language they can comfortably forward.

Add tools to support the habit

Use a CRM and configured reminders to organize activity. Review your message and audience before turning on campaigns.

02 / THE CONVERSATION

A better question
opens a better door.

Stay with the business-owner conversation here. Use the Partner Program for professional alliances.

CHOOSE A STARTING POINT

Operating costs

“Which business expense would you most like to understand better this year?”

Ask what they have already reviewed, who helps them evaluate options, and what would make a second look worthwhile.

  1. Notice something real

    Mention a shared connection, their work, or an actual conversation. Avoid generic praise.

  2. Ask one open question

    Make it easy to answer. Give them room to explain what matters.

  3. Listen and reflect

    “It sounds like [priority] is what you want to improve. Did I understand that?”

  4. Offer one relevant next step

    Ask permission to share an overview or introduce a specialist. Agree on when to reconnect.

03 / KEEP YOUR WORD

Follow-up with a reason.
Make every touch useful.

This is a sample rhythm to adapt to the conversation, not a required sequence or automatic campaign.

AFTER YOU MEET

Send what you promised.

Reference the conversation and provide the specific resource.

A FEW DAYS LATER

Make room for questions.

If appropriate, ask whether it was relevant and offer to clarify.

WHEN YOU HAVE VALUE

Share a useful detail.

Send something that directly relates to the priority they mentioned.

AT THE AGREED TIME

Decide the next step.

Continue, reconnect later, or close the loop. Respect a request to stop.

04 / FIND YOUR WORDS

Keep the opening simple.
Let their answer lead.

These drafts focus on business owners and decision-makers. Personalize every placeholder.

A little context makes it yours.

The drafts update as you type. Nothing is saved or sent.

START WITH THE RELATIONSHIP

Hi [First Name], [Shared Detail] came to mind, and I wanted to check in. How are things going with the business? I’m working with Dreams Business Resources, connecting owners with resources and specialists around business costs, benefits, and growth. I’d enjoy hearing what you’re focused on. [Your Name]

Reconnect as a person first. Only describe an offer when it fits the conversation.

ASK PERMISSION

Hi [First Name], I’m [Your Name] with Dreams Business Resources. I noticed [Shared Detail]. We help connect business owners with resources to review areas such as expenses, employee benefits, and potential tax-credit opportunities. Is there an area you are looking to improve, or someone on your team who handles those reviews?

Avoid quoting a savings figure or implying eligibility before an appropriate review.

FOLLOW THROUGH

Hi [First Name], I’m following up on the [resource] we discussed about [Shared Detail]. Was it relevant to what you are working on? If so, we can decide whether a short conversation with the right specialist would be useful. If the timing is off, just let me know. [Your Name]

A useful follow-up has context, a clear question, and room for the person to decline.
