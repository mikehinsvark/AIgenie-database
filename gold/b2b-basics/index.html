01 / THE ORIGINAL PROSPECTING ROUTINE

Six habits.
One connected routine.

The original Fast Start lesson combines digital marketing, social posts, local visits, chambers, a contact database, and referrals. Put each one to work with a clear next step.

A / DIGITAL MARKETING

Explore a digital marketing package

The original lesson points to Dreams Cloud 3. Compare the current Dreams Business Cloud options with your audience, budget, and follow-up plan. Explore Dreams Business Cloud →

B / SHOW UP CONSISTENTLY

Post on social media 2–3 days a week

Use Facebook and LinkedIn to answer business-owner questions, share a useful resource, or explain a common business challenge. Choose a repeatable schedule and review what starts real conversations.

C / LOCAL OUTREACH

Distribute flyers & Connect Cards

Plan local visits on Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday; add a fourth day if it fits your week. Start with industrial parks, strip malls, and office buildings. Offer a card with a brief introduction. Order Connect Cards ↗

D / BUILD LOCAL RELATIONSHIPS

Join your local Chamber

Review the membership options and event calendar. Attend mixers, breakfasts, and relevant member events; become a familiar face and follow up on the conversations you start. Check local dues and guest policies. Use the chamber playbook →

E / ORGANIZE & FOLLOW THROUGH

Build your database & use Slybroadcast

Keep names, companies, relationship notes, and next steps together. The original training pairs the database with email, video, and Slybroadcast ringless voicemail. Choose the right channel for each contact and confirm permission and setup before sending.

F–G / REFERRALS & FOLLOW-UP

Create a referral system + follow up

After delivering value, make it easy for a satisfied client to introduce two or three relevant owners. Give them a short description they can share, thank them, and close the loop. Keep following through on the next step you agreed. Build your referral routine →

02 / A WEEK YOU CAN REPEAT

A little structure.
A lot more intention.

A suggested rhythm inspired by the Fast Start lesson. Adjust the pace to your availability and the people you serve.

MON

Prepare your warm list

Choose a few owners you know. Add their business, how you know them, one useful detail, and a reason to reconnect.

TUE–THU

Make room for real conversations

Plan short visits to a business district or attend a chamber event. Bring Connect Cards and leave time to record what you learned.

2–3× / WK

Share something useful

Post a helpful answer, a practical checklist, or a lesson from your work on LinkedIn or Facebook. Keep private client details out.

DAILY

Deliver what you promised

Send the agreed resource, answer a question, or confirm a meeting. Follow the person’s preferred channel and timing.

FRI

Review and improve

Look at conversations, replies, and agreed next steps. Notice what helped, update your notes, and plan the next week.

03 / THE CONVERSATION

A better question
opens a better door.

Stay with the business-owner conversation here. Use the Partner Program for professional alliances.

CHOOSE A STARTING POINT

Operating costs

“Which business expense would you most like to understand better this year?”

Ask what they have already reviewed, who helps them evaluate options, and what would make a second look worthwhile.

  1. Notice something real

    Mention a shared connection, their work, or an actual conversation. Avoid generic praise.

  2. Ask one open question

    Make it easy to answer. Give them room to explain what matters.

  3. Listen and reflect

    “It sounds like [priority] is what you want to improve. Did I understand that?”

  4. Offer one relevant next step

    Ask permission to share an overview or introduce a specialist. Agree on when to reconnect.

05 / FIND YOUR WORDS

Keep the opening simple.
Let their answer lead.

These drafts focus on business owners and decision-makers. Personalize every placeholder.

A little context makes it yours.

The drafts update as you type. Nothing is saved or sent.

START WITH THE RELATIONSHIP

Hi [First Name], [Shared Detail] came to mind, and I wanted to check in. How are things going with the business? I’m working with Dreams Business Resources, connecting owners with resources and specialists around business costs, benefits, and growth. I’d enjoy hearing what you’re focused on. [Your Name]

Reconnect as a person first. Only describe an offer when it fits the conversation.

ASK PERMISSION

Hi [First Name], I’m [Your Name] with Dreams Business Resources. I noticed [Shared Detail]. We help connect business owners with resources to review areas such as expenses, employee benefits, and potential tax-credit opportunities. Is there an area you are looking to improve, or someone on your team who handles those reviews?

Avoid quoting a savings figure or implying eligibility before an appropriate review.

FOLLOW THROUGH

Hi [First Name], I’m following up on the [resource] we discussed about [Shared Detail]. Was it relevant to what you are working on? If so, we can decide whether a short conversation with the right specialist would be useful. If the timing is off, just let me know. [Your Name]

A useful follow-up has context, a clear question, and room for the person to decline.

Turn the original numbers into a workload plan.

300 contacts × 12 touches = 3,600 planned touches. That is an activity example, not 3,600 replies or sales. Space contact around relevance, responses, and the person’s preferences.
