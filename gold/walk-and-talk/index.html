01 / A PLAN YOU CAN FOLLOW

A few good stops.
A reason to return.

Start with a compact area within about five miles. These six settings come from the original lesson; choose times that respect how each business operates.

LOCAL SHOPS & SERVICES

Strip malls

Visit restaurants, salons, and retail shops outside their busiest periods. Learn about the business before introducing a resource.

MANUFACTURERS & CONTRACTORS

Industrial parks

Look for manufacturers, contractors, and service companies. Start at reception and ask who handles operating costs, finance, or employee benefits.

HR, PAYROLL & ACCOUNTING

Office buildings

Meet HR, payroll, and accounting firms. Clarify whether the conversation concerns their own business or a separate professional alliance.

PRACTICES & PROFESSIONAL TEAMS

Medical & dental plazas

Ask for the practice manager and a suitable time for a business introduction. Keep the conversation focused on the practice’s business needs.

OWNERS IN YOUR COMMUNITY

Chamber of Commerce events

Use the local calendar to find mixers, breakfasts, and member events. Learn who is in the room and ask what they are working on.

REPEAT VISITS & WARM INTRODUCTIONS

BNI & networking breakfasts

Explore a local chapter or networking group, check its guest rules, and meet complementary professionals who can make relevant introductions.

02 / THE ORIGINAL WALK & TALK SCHEDULE

Three days to connect.
A daily habit of follow-through.

Use the original activity targets as a starting plan. A visit is not automatically a new contact; record what actually happened.

3 WALK DAYS / WEEK

Tuesday–Thursday

Protect a three- to four-hour visit block. The source suggests 10 a.m.–2 p.m.; adjust around local opening times and busy service periods.

30 CONTACTS / WEEK TARGET

10 new contacts per walk day

Prepare enough stops to work toward ten new conversations. Three days at that activity target equals 30 new contacts per week.

CONTEXT + A NEXT STEP

Follow up within 24 hours

Use the LinkedIn or email follow-up you agreed. Send the resource you promised and record the result immediately in GHL / Dreams Cloud or your own database.

MAKE EACH STOP COUNT

A short introduction.
A useful next step.

  1. Pre-plan the route

    Group nearby businesses and check hours before leaving. Keep time afterward for notes and follow-up.

  2. Offer a Connect Card

    If the owner is busy, ask whether you may leave a card or return another time. Explain where the card leads.

  3. Log the context immediately

    Record the name, company, location, business need, preferred contact method, and promised next action.

TOPICS FROM THE ORIGINAL LESSON

Connect the need
with the right resource.

  • Restaurants, salons & retail: the source mentions FICA Tip and Bank Breezy.
  • Manufacturers & contractors: the source mentions R&D and Bank Breezy.
  • HR, payroll & accounting: the source mentions Section 125 and Quantum.
  • Medical & dental practices: the source mentions R&D credits and Quantum Health.

These are conversation topics from the source, not eligibility findings. Start with the actual business need and have the appropriate specialist review current program requirements.

Understand the original “$3M game plan” example

The source uses 10 new contacts per day for 3 days, a 10% appointment rate, and a 33% close rate. These are planning assumptions, not measured results.

  • 10 × 3 = 30 contacts per week.
  • 30 × 10% = 3 appointments per week.
  • 3 × 33% = 0.99 clients per week, rounded to one in the source.
  • The move from a $250,000 R&D credit to a $25,000 commission implies a 10% commission assumption; the credit amount is not itself income.
  • Using the unrounded 0.99 gives $1,287,000 over 52 weeks; rounding to one gives $1.3 million. That calculation does not establish the $3 million headline.

Keep financial projections separate from activity goals. Use actual conversion data and current written compensation terms; no income or tax-credit result is promised.

03 / WHEN YOU ARRIVE

Respect their time.
Earn the next conversation.

A visit is an introduction, not a full presentation. Pay attention to the person and the setting.

  1. Ask if it is a good time

    Introduce yourself and ask whether they have a moment. If they are busy, offer to return.

  2. Learn who handles the topic

    Ask who looks after business costs, benefits, or the priority they mention. Be courteous to everyone.

  3. Offer one useful resource

    Share a Connect Card or overview with permission. Ask how they would prefer you to follow up.

  4. Record the context

    Note the name, business, one genuine detail, and agreed next action. Follow through promptly.

04 / A NATURAL INTRODUCTION

Brief. Curious.
Easy to respond to.

Use the words that fit your personality. Keep the request small and specific.

A little context makes it yours.

The drafts update as you type. Nothing is saved or sent.

WHEN YOU WALK IN

Hi, I’m [Your Name] with Dreams Business Resources. Is this a good time for a quick introduction? We help business owners review potential tax-credit opportunities, payroll costs, and employee benefits with the appropriate specialists. Would a 15-minute introductory conversation be useful, or is there someone else here who handles those reviews?

Ask permission first. If they prefer no visits or follow-up, respect that.

LEAVE THE DOOR OPEN

Of course — I can see you’re busy. Would it be okay to leave a Connect Card? If the topic is relevant, is there a better day or way to make an introduction? Thank you, [Your Name]

Do not insist on a name, contact detail, or a card handoff. A courteous exit is a good outcome.

AFTER THE VISIT

Hi [First Name], Thanks for taking a moment when I stopped by [Business]. I appreciated hearing about [Shared Detail]. Here is the [resource] we discussed. Would [agreed next step] still be useful? [Your Name]

Replace the business, resource, and next-step placeholders with what you actually discussed.
