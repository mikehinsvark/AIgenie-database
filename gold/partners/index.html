03 / BUILD THE RELATIONSHIP

Start with one client.
Make the process clear.

A small, well-managed introduction gives both sides a way to learn.

  1. Learn about their clients

    Ask whom they help, what those clients ask for, and where complementary support would be welcome.

  2. Agree on a useful introduction

    Choose a clear client profile and a relevant concern. Request the client’s permission before sharing details.

  3. Bring in the right specialist

    Define the scope of the review and the information the specialist needs.

  4. Discuss what the review found

    Keep findings specific to the client and let the client decide whether to proceed.

  5. Close the loop

    Thank the partner and share an appropriate status update. Review the experience before expanding.

THE ALLIANCE CONVERSATION

Make the expectations
as clear as the opportunity.

  • Who is an appropriate introduction?
  • How will you ask for the client’s permission?
  • Who owns the next action and follows up?
  • Which services require licensed or qualified professionals?
  • What, if any, compensation is allowed and documented?

Referral compensation depends on the program, signed agreement, professional obligations, and applicable rules. Agree on the terms before discussing a payment with a client or partner.

04 / FIND YOUR WORDS

Lead with their clients.
Explore the fit together.

Five moments in a thoughtful partner conversation.

A little context makes it yours.

The drafts update as you type. Nothing is saved or sent.

THE INITIAL CONVERSATION

Hi [First Name], I noticed [Shared Detail]. I work with Dreams Business Resources, and I’m interested in learning about the business owners you serve. Would you be open to a short conversation about whether our resources could complement the work you already do? [Your Name]

Ask about their practice before describing the entire program.

UNDERSTAND THE NEED

Which business questions do your clients bring to you that fall outside your core service? When a client needs help in one of those areas, how do you usually decide whom to introduce?

Listen for the standard you would need to meet to earn their trust.

A SMALL FIRST STEP

If we find a relevant resource for a client, would you be comfortable asking whether they would like an introduction? We can agree on the scope and how you stay informed before moving ahead.

The partner and client choose whether an introduction is welcome.

PROTECT THE RELATIONSHIP

It sounds like protecting your client relationships is important. What would you need to see or understand for a small first introduction to feel comfortable?

Acknowledge the concern. Avoid promising that the partner will have no work or no risk.

KEEP IT PRACTICAL

Would it be useful to look at a sample Dreams Score overview together and decide whether one client comes to mind? If there is a fit, we can clarify the process and current program terms before taking the next step.

Keep the first commitment small and follow through on it.
